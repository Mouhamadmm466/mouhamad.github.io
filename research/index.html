Research;

  • Zarma speech technology from scarce data Namu AI Lab, 2026paperRecognition, synthesis, translation and a voice agent for Zarma, built from the roughly 1% of its public audio that is open and transcribed. About a third of the measured error was spelling, not hearing.
  • Toward spoken conversation in Hausa Namu AI Lab, 2026paperA full-duplex model learned the timing of conversation (95.6% of turn ends answered on held-out synthetic dialogue, median 0.78 s) but not what to say. So we built an ear and a mouth around a frozen language model.
  • One speech synthesis model for Hausa, Zarma and French Namu AI Lab, 2026paperOne voice-cloning model grown from Hausa to Zarma to French, measured at each step for what every new language cost the others. Trained jointly, it holds all three within tolerances fixed in advance.
  • What open language models know of Hausa, Zarma and Fulfulde Namu AI Lab, 2026paperWhat open language models read, understand and write in three languages of Niger. One trained on African languages reads Hausa, understands some Zarma and writes none, so our Zarma agent thinks in French.
  • Measuring Hausa speech recognition Namu AI Lab, 2026paperSpelling conventions and test subsets move a Hausa error rate about as much as switching between two same-size recognisers. In FLEURS, half the word types written with a hooked letter also appear without it.
  • Collecting consented speech in the languages of the Sahel Namu AI Lab, 2026paperPublic speech in Niger's languages comes from a handful of speakers; a recogniser trained on five memorised their voices. So we designed a programme to record speech, with consent, with the people who speak them.
  • Compressing transformer language models research assistant, CWRUWith a PhD student at Case Western: a PyTorch framework that trains, prunes and benchmarks transformers across 300+ configurations. The best made models up to 70% smaller within 1% of the original accuracy.
  • Predicting financial stress 1st place, ZindigithubFirst place and $3,000 for predicting which mobile money customers will run short of cash in the next 30 days, from six months of their behaviour.
