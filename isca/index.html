a personal exploration

Isca

Exploring how AI and software can help us build smarter, more reliable wireless networks.

01 age eight

I was eight when I first wondered how two devices could talk without a wire between them. So I started a small project to find out how information could travel through something I couldn't even see.

I didn't know about protocols or radio frequencies. I didn't know how the internet worked. I was just curious, and like most things I built at that age, I ended up with more questions than answers.

02 going back

One thing I have come to love about learning is going back to something you once tried to build, this time knowing how it works. The idea often stays the same. What changes is what you know, and what you can do with it.

03 this summer

This summer I spent time on research in AI and wireless networking. I learned how wireless networks behave: how they handle interference and traffic, and how software and machine learning can help them perform better.

Somewhere along the way, I started thinking about that little project again.

04 the question

So I am picking it up again, with a much bigger goal.

I want to find out whether AI and software can design, run and improve wireless networks. Can a network learn from its own conditions, see a problem before it happens, and make better decisions about how it should work?

05 the plan

I am starting with a simulated wireless network, where I can try different setups, traffic patterns and interference.

Next, I want to build software that watches how the network performs, and test AI models that help choose channels, ease congestion and share resources. Then I want to take what works in simulation and try it on real wireless hardware.

06 scale

What interests me most is scale: hundreds or thousands of access points working together, without someone adjusting them by hand.

07 still exploring

This is still an exploration. I don't know exactly where it will lead, and that is part of what makes it interesting.

The question that got me started hasn't changed. I just know more now, I have better tools, and I get to explore it further.
